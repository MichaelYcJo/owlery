# Name: Owlery

*English · [한국어](./name.ko.md)*

## Meaning

An owlery is an abode of owls, or an enclosure where owls are kept ([Wiktionary "owlery"](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/owlery); the entry cites Webster's 1913 dictionary). The word is old: Thomas Carlyle used it in *Latter-Day Pamphlets* (1850).

## Why this name

- **The owls are the agents.** AgenticSeal's emblem is a small owl on a red wax seal. The owl is Athene noctua, the little owl that is the bird of the goddess Athena. An owlery is where many such owls live and come back to: agents that work as one team, and the inbox where their work returns.
- **The emblem is the owl, and the wax stays with the seal.** The name says where the work gathers. Owlery's emblem is a warm white owl on a black rounded square: the little owl of AgenticSeal's seal, taken out of its wax, with no red on it. The app's icon and the README show it. An icon candidate is judged at the size the Dock draws it. The owl on the red wax seal stays AgenticSeal's mark of a guarantee and says what leaves: work that a party independent of the one that built it has judged and sealed. The window draws it at a seal's place and nowhere else, and red stands on the seal alone. The owner settled the emblem on 2026-11-19, to tell the product's face from the seal. Keeping the owl in the wax seal and drawing the owl in front of an owlery were the two options left open, and neither was taken.
- **It reads one way.** English and Korean speakers say it the same way at first sight.
- **The name stays Owlery.** The owner decided it on 2026-11-19, in the rebranding that weighed the name again with the study of the window's look in hand. An owlery is where the owls come back to, and the window now opens on the inbox, the place the work comes back to, so the name says what the first screen is.

- **Harry Potter.** Many readers will first think of the owlery at Hogwarts. The word predates the books, but the product's screens and promotion do not borrow images from them, such as owls carrying letters.

- **The name is taken on PyPI.** The npm and Homebrew names are free. A Python package would need another name.
- **A brand name is searched against these criteria before it is proposed.**
  - Its meaning is read from a dictionary's own entry, such as Wiktionary's, and a meaning not read there is marked as one from memory.
  - English and Korean speakers read it one way at first sight, and it sounds the same in its source language and in English.
  - It runs to about three syllables, and its ending does not sound like a person's name. Endings such as -os, -a and -ia sound like a name, and endings such as -ic, -al, -on and -um sound like a concept.
  - Its domains are looked up under .com, .dev, .app, .ai, .sh, .io and .co, and each look-up states its date.
  - The same and similar names are searched on GitHub, npm and the web. A product of the same field with the same or a near spelling rules the name out, and so does a software trademark (class 42) with a near spelling.
  - Before the name is taken, its trademarks are searched as the bullet *Trademarks have not been checked* above says, and the names of its GitHub organization and its social accounts are checked.
