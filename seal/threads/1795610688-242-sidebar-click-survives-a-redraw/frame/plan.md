# Implementation Plan: 1795610688-242-sidebar-click-survives-a-redraw

## Technical context

The tree as read at `80917d43`.

The defect's cause is shown, by the code and by a probe (`frame/design.md` *Grounding*, *What the probes showed*, probe 1). `sidebar.ts#showSidebar` (line 152) replaces every element under `#repositories` at each draw. In Electron 45, a press whose element leaves the document before the release fires no `click` anywhere. `renderer.ts#takeLists` (lines 1553 to 1566) draws the sidebar, the list in front, the view, the summary and six dialogs at each `inbox`. The `accounts` case (lines 1806 to 1808) and the `machine` case (lines 1827 to 1829) repeat the first three of those draws. The list screens (`list.ts` lines 201 and 212) and the summary (`summary.ts` line 69) replace their lines the same way, at the same messages.

The chosen approach does not change how anything is drawn. It changes when a daemon message's draw runs: while the mouse's main button is held down in the window, the draw waits, and it runs in a task after the release (probe 2). The state the message carries is taken at once, and the notifications are raised at once. Every draw the daemon's lists cause goes through one function, so the hold covers the sidebar, the list screens, the summary, the view and the dialogs that name what the daemon lists together. The Accounts dialog at an `accounts` and the Machine dialog at a `machine` go through the hold as draws of their own (`frame/design.md` *Scope* item 2, after round 1's finding 2).

The hold's logic stands in a module of its own, `apps/desktop/src/renderer/press.ts`. `view.ts` is the module of the renderer's pure functions, but issues #214 and #215 change it in this release, and the hold reads nothing of it. The unit project runs in Electron's Node with no DOM (`apps/desktop/vitest.config.ts`, `scripts/test-in-electron.mjs`), so the module touches no DOM when imported. Its wiring takes the window as an argument.

The new built-app case goes into `apps/desktop/test/seal-back-view.test.ts`. That file already opens a client socket to the daemon (lines 99 to 112), and a `setWorkItem` there makes the daemon send an `inbox` to every client (`apps/desktop/src/daemon/daemon.ts` line 1212). The case cannot see the window take that `inbox` through the drawing, since the hold keeps the drawing back. So it adds a listener to the window's socket through CDP `Runtime.queryObjects` (probe 3). `BuiltApp` exposes `cdp` (`apps/desktop/test/built-app.ts`), and the probe used the same CDP calls. No hook is added to the app for the case.

**Failure scenario of the chosen approach: what breaks in six months.** A later change draws the daemon's lists by calling `showSidebar` or a list's `show` straight from a new message case, outside the one function. #216's refresh and #227's worktrees are the likely ones. The click on a line is then lost again at that message, and the built-app case still passes, since it sends an `inbox`. The docstring of the one function and the rule of `frame/design.md` *Rule changes* name the path every such draw takes. A second, smaller cost: a press held for seconds, such as a selection dragged in a terminal pane, delays the sidebar's catching up by that long. The lists are taken and notified at once, and only their drawing waits.

## Alternatives considered

| Approach | Failure scenario | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Hold a daemon message's draws while the main button is held, and draw in a task after the release** (chosen) | A new message that draws outside the one function bypasses the hold (*Technical context*). A long press, such as a drag in a terminal, delays the lists' drawing until its release. | Chosen. Probe 2 shows the click reaching the pressed button. It changes no drawing code, so #215, #227 and #228 change what the sidebar draws without meeting it. It covers every line and button that the lists' draws replace, not only the sidebar's. |
| Keep the structure: `showSidebar` replaces its children at each draw, and every other piece of drawing code stays as it is | This is the structure the chosen approach keeps. Its premise is that a full redraw is correct at every draw except one made during a press. Probe 1 checks that premise: the click is lost only because the pressed element leaves the document, and a draw deferred past the click loses nothing. | Kept, with the hold in front of it. |
| Replace the structure: keep each line's element across draws, keyed by its work item, and update its parts in place | Probe 1: a kept button that is moved into a new `li`, or whose pressed child is replaced, still loses the click. A reorder when a state changes, a pin, or #228's tree moves lines, so only an update that never moves or replaces the pressed node holds. That is a rewrite of every part a line draws, and it meets #215, #227 and #228 in `sidebar.ts`. | Rejected. |
| Listen for `click` on `#repositories` and find the line from the event, which the triage named | Probe 1: no `click` reaches `#repositories` or `document` once the pressed element is gone. | Rejected, by the probe. |
| Pair the press and the release by work item: note the line under `pointerdown`, and open it at `pointerup` where the line under the release is the same | Where no draw falls in between, a `click` follows the `pointerup` too, so every listener has to tell the two apart. A keyboard's Enter and Space fire `click` alone, and assistive technology may fire either. Each control of each list needs the pairing, the repository row's buttons too. The pressed ground (`:active`) still vanishes at the draw. | Rejected: more code at every control, and the pressed state stays broken. |
| Put the hold inside `showSidebar` and each list's `show` | It covers a future caller of `showSidebar` without the docstring's help. But it also defers the person's own draws, such as the fold head's `showSidebar` followed by a `focus()` on the new head (`sidebar.ts#showSidebar`, the fold head's `click` listener), wherever a key is pressed during a press. It means edits in `sidebar.ts`, `list.ts`, `summary.ts` and `work-item-view.ts`, which #215, #224 and #228 change in this release. | Rejected. The hold stands at the one place where the daemon's draws begin. |
| Hold the whole message, its state and its draws, while a press is held | A `workItemCreated` or a `done` that follows a held `inbox` runs against lists the window has not taken yet. The protocol orders a `done` before the `inbox` that carries its change (`apps/desktop/src/shared/protocol.ts`, the comment of the `reverseDecision` message). A pane's `output` cannot wait during a drag in the terminal. | Rejected. Only the draws wait. |
| Keep the Accounts dialog and the Machine dialog drawn at once at their messages, and narrow the rule's sentence to the lists' draws (round 1, finding 2, the fix it wrote) | `renderer.ts#showAccounts` replaces each account's row with its buttons and its rename field, so a press on "Rename", "Remove…" or "Make the default" while another client changes an account loses its click, as a sidebar line did. The rule would then state an exception the person meets as the same defect. | Rejected. The two dialogs join the hold, one draw each, and the rule states no exception. |
| Run the deferred draw in a microtask at the release | Probe 2: the microtask runs before the `click` is dispatched, so the draw replaces the pressed element first and the click is lost. | Rejected, by the probe. |
