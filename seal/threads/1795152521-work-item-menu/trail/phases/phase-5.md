**The two things the orchestrator asked this phase to decide.**

- **A delete and an archive never end one work item's sessions at once.** Review round 4 noted that a delete runs on `Daemon#changing` and an archive does not. The daemon now holds each work item a delete is under way for in `Daemon#deleting`, from the delete's first read of the loss until it removed the work item or was refused. A removal that deletes holds every work item of its repository there for as long as it runs.
  - An archive of a work item in `Daemon#deleting` is refused with "work item <id> is being deleted", before it ends anything.
  - A delete of a work item whose archive is ending its sessions is refused with "An archive of work item <id> is ending its sessions. Try again.", before it reads or ends anything. A removal that deletes is refused the same way before its first delete.
  - Grounds: each of the two stops the headless sessions under words of its own and then waits, so whichever came second would leave the first one's words on a session it did not stop, or archive a work item whose folder git is removing. Refusing the second costs one sentence and a second press. Putting the archive on the chain was the other way, and `trail/phases/phase-4.md` says why an archive does not wait behind a creation. A removal without the delete keeps what round 4 read: it can take a work item whose archive waits, and the archive is then answered "no work item <id>".
  - Judged against *Scope* items 17, 21 and 23. Two cases of `test/daemon.test.ts` hold it, one for each order.
- **What a headless session a delete stopped says.** A delete can be refused after its stop: a pane outlives the wait, the loss changed while the sessions ended, or git refuses. The session is `failed` with "ended when a delete of its work item began", which holds whether or not the delete then removes the work item. A removal that deletes stops each work item's sessions inside that work item's delete, so they say the same words, and a removal without the delete keeps "ended when a removal of its repository began". Judged against review round 3's finding 2. A case holds the words on a delete that is refused after the stop.
