## Scope

In scope:

1. **A pane's process that has ended leaves no pseudo-terminal open in the process that started it.** `node-pty@1.1.0` is patched through pnpm's `patchedDependencies` in the root `package.json`, with the patch under `patches/` and its hash in `pnpm-lock.yaml`. The patch changes `src/unix/pty.cc`: in `pty_posix_spawn`, after the spawn, it closes `slave` where it was opened and every descriptor of `low_fds` from index 0 to `count`; and in `PtyFork`, where the spawn failed, it closes `master` before it throws `posix_spawnp failed.`. `node-pty@1.2.0-beta.18` does both. The app's `postinstall` builds that source for Electron, and node-pty loads `build/Release` before its prebuilt binary (`lib/utils.js`, read).
2. **Two cases pin item 1.** `apps/desktop/test/pty.test.ts` gains two cases. The second calls node-pty's native `fork` with a spawn helper that does not exist, expects each call to throw `posix_spawnp failed.`, and finds the process's pseudo-terminal descriptors as they were before. The first it reads which descriptors of its own process name a pseudo-terminal device (`/dev/ptmx`, `/dev/ttys…`, or `(revoked)`, the name `lsof` gives a slave whose master has closed), starts several `TerminalSession`s, some that end by themselves and some ended by `kill()`, waits for each one's exit, and then reads the same list again and finds it as it was before.
3. **The fetch case of #202 holds what it guards whenever git starts its transport.** `worktrees.test.ts`, "gives up a remote that does not answer within the wait, and ends the process git started for it and leaves no lock": the case runs the fetch with a wait of 2 seconds. Where the stand-in recorded no start, it asserts what holds without one (the result with that wait's sentence, the time against that wait, no lock) and runs the fetch again with a wait three times as long, three attempts at most. In the attempt where the stand-in recorded a start, it asserts all it asserts today. Where no attempt started the stand-in, the case fails. The case states its own limit, which covers the three waits.
4. **The daemon's case of the same shape does the same.** `daemon.test.ts`, "has a creation join the fetch of its base that is under way: one fetch runs, and the work item is made after its answer": each attempt has its own clone and its own daemon, whose `baseFetchMs` is the attempt's wait, and asserts the order of the messages and the `baseFetched` answer; the attempt whose stand-in recorded a start asserts one start and its end.
5. **The stand-in of #258's case writes its error text before its first line.** `claude-adapter.test.ts`, "fails a role launch whose session %s: an error result with the reason, the CLI stopped, and nothing else of the run but its exit": the script CLI prints `still here` on its error stream, then its lines, then sleeps for 30 seconds. The expected events stay as they are, `errorOutput: "still here\n"` among them, for both rows of the `it.each`.
6. **The cases of the inbox that compare the whole list of inboxes start their daemon with a hello that waits for the first read of the commits.** In `describe("the inbox")` of `daemon.test.ts`, each case that compares `inboxes(…)` with a whole list and whose daemon's store lists a work item at a hello gives its daemon `readyWaitMs: 5000`, the value the file's other cases set for the same reason (`:3848`, `:3977`, `:4097`). A case that already sets the option, and a case that tests the wait itself, stays as it is.
7. **The give-up of the account-change group's waits says why it holds.** The comment at `client#until` and the one at `holdingShell#launches` each say that the 5 seconds is a give-up and not a wait, and give the longest wait the group showed in item 8's run under load, with its date.
8. **#241's last two rows are answered by a run under load.** The phase record states, for the stream case and the two role launch cases of `claude-adapter.test.ts` and for the case of `settle.test.ts` that row 1795398267 named, the times they took in three runs beside eight `yes` loops, against their bounds.
9. **The rule of item 1 is written into the policy** (`## Rule changes` below).

Out of scope:

- A move to `node-pty@1.2.0`. It is a prerelease at this commit, and `plan.md` *Alternatives considered* says why the patch was chosen. The patch goes when the dependency moves to a version that holds the fix, and item 2's case then pins the new version.
- Any change of `apps/desktop/src`. The cause of #202 and #258 is in the cases, and the cause of #300 is in the dependency.
- A longer give-up for the suite's waits. Nothing read shows a wait that ran out for time.
- The hosted runner's present failures, #207 and #244, the `check` job's cancellation, #296, and the suite's long files, #200. Each has its issue.
- The other waits `gaps.md` of `1795710118-291-292-295-three-cases-hold-under-load` names under *Not done*.

## Data & interfaces

No schema, message or option of the product changes. The root `package.json` gains `pnpm.patchedDependencies` with the one key `node-pty@1.1.0`, and `pnpm-lock.yaml` records the patch's hash, so `pnpm install --frozen-lockfile` applies it on every machine and on the runner.
