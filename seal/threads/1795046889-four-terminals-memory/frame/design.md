## Scope

1. **A work item's view shows up to four of its panes at once.** The pane bar gains one control, "Side by side", a button that is pressed or not. The owner chose it (`frame/questions.md` Q1). The layout for one orchestrator with parallel subtasks is issue #35's.
   - Not pressed, the stage holds the terminal of the tab in front, as today.
   - Pressed, the stage holds the work item's panes as tiles, each a terminal fitted to its tile. Two panes stand in two columns. Three or four stand in two columns and two rows, and the third of three spans both columns. A work item with more tabs than four shows four of them, in the tabs' order: the pane in front, and then the panes of the view's stage order, up to four. A view's stage order is the panes that were in front in that view and are still its tabs, the most recent first, and then its tabs that were never in front, the highest id first. After a load no pane was in front, so the stage holds the pane in front and the three tabs with the highest ids besides it.
   - The tab of every pane on the stage is marked as shown, and the tab of the pane that has the keyboard is marked as in front. A press on a tab that is not on the stage puts its pane there, in place of the shown pane least recently in front, and a pane this window starts comes onto the stage the same way. A press inside a tile gives that pane the keyboard.
   - The tiles share the stage as it stands, between the sidebar and the view's right column. Whether the right column gives way while the control is pressed is `frame/questions.md` Q4, and this item is its default.
   - Whether the control is pressed is the window's own state, one for every view, off at each load.
   - A session that starts to wait is not notified while its pane is on the stage of a focused window, whether or not it has the keyboard. `notificationFor` decides as today, and its caller passes that as `inFront`.
2. **A terminal screen exists only for a pane on the stage, and is reused.** A screen is one `Terminal` with its `FitAddon`, its `WebglAddon` and its element. The window holds a pool of them.
   - A pane that goes onto the stage takes a free screen, and a new one is made only where none is free. The pool therefore never holds more screens than the most panes that were on the stage at once, which item 1 caps at four.
   - A pane that leaves the stage gives its screen back: the window sends `detach` for it, resets the terminal and hides the element. A pane leaves the stage when another tab takes its place, when "Side by side" is released, when the view of another work item opens, when the summary, the inbox or the history screen comes in front, and when it is closed.
   - A pane that takes a screen is attached as a new terminal is attached today: the screen is fitted, the process is told the size, the window sends `attach`, and the replay is written into the reset terminal.
   - What a person types into a screen, and each size the screen takes, goes to the pane that holds the screen at that moment. A free screen sends nothing. What the terminal answers to a question a replay holds is sent only for the first replay the window draws of a pane since it loaded. While the terminal draws a later replay, or still draws what it was handed before the pane on the screen took it, the screen sends nothing a person types or the terminal answers. A size it takes still goes to its pane where that pane's process runs.
   - A free screen stays in the pool, hidden, for the window's life. The measurement reports what the pool holds while the inbox is in front (item 6).
3. **A hidden pane's output stays with the daemon.** The protocol gains the client message `detach`. After it, the daemon sends that client no output of that session until the client attaches again. The session, its process and its kept output are untouched. A `detach` for a session the client does not receive, or that the daemon does not list, changes nothing and is answered with nothing.
4. **A pane shown again draws its output once.** Where a pane leaves the stage and returns before the first replay arrived, two replays are on their way. The window writes the last one it asked for and no other, and drops output that arrives before it, as `work-item-view.ts#write` drops it for one replay today.
5. **An exited pane keeps its tab and its last screen without the window holding a terminal for it.**
   - When a session's process exits, the daemon appends the line `[process exited with code <N>]`, on a line of its own, to the session's kept output and sends it as `output` to the clients that receive the session. A replay of an exited session therefore ends with it. The window writes nothing of its own at `exit`.
   - A pane a client closed while its process ran gets no exit line: it is on no list, and its end is told to nobody.
   - The tabs of a work item's view are its sessions as the daemon lists them, alive or exited, in the order of their ids. An exited pane shows a tab after a reload as well, where today it has none unless the window holds its terminal, and its close control takes it off the daemon's lists.
   - A replay opens at the start of a line. Where `OutputBuffer` drops old output, it also drops the rest of the line the cut falls in, so what it keeps never opens inside an escape sequence that stands on one line. Where what it keeps holds no line break within its first 4,096 characters, the cut stays where the count of characters puts it, so a program that prints a long stretch without a line break, as a full-screen program may, keeps its output and loses at most 4,096 characters to the cut.
6. **The measurement is in the tree and starts the app through `BuiltApp.start`.** It is run as `pnpm run measure:memory` from `apps/desktop`, which sets `OWLERY_MEASURE_MEMORY=1` and runs `test/memory.test.ts` in Electron's Node, as `korean:typed` runs the typed run. It is macOS's alone, as v0.1 is. Without the variable the file runs the cases of its own functions and starts no app, so `pnpm run check` and the `Broad gate` take no measurement.
   - It builds `dist/` from the tree, as the Korean case does, and starts the app with a user data folder and a worktrees folder of its own. It makes work items and shell panes through the window's own controls, as `korean-pane.ts#openReceiverPane` makes one, and reads the daemon's process id from that run folder.
   - A run an agent makes sets `OWLERY_BACKGROUND=1` in the command's environment. The app then takes no focus, and the measurement sends `Emulation.setFocusEmulationEnabled` so that the page draws (`## Grounding`, the probes). A run a person makes leaves the variable unset, and the app starts as it ships: the measurement passes `keepBackground` and sends no emulation. Each table says which kind of start its run was.
   - Each pane's process is a fixture that prints about 300 lines a second of text with colour escapes, the rate of the earlier measurement, from before the first sample to the last. It is the app's `SHELL`, as `fixtures/receiver.sh` is the Korean case's.
   - It takes a sample of the sum of `phys_footprint`, read with `footprint`, 20, 25 and 30 seconds after the scenario's last step. At each sample it reads from the page `document.visibilityState`, how many frames the page drew in one second, and how many terminals and canvases the page holds and how many of the terminals are displayed. A run that holds a sample taken while the page was hidden or drew no frame is thrown away, and in a person's run so is one taken while the window did not have the focus.
   - The app itself is the Electron main process, every process below it, and the daemon. The processes below the daemon are the panes' and are summed apart: the fixtures here, and an agent's CLI in use.
   - Its scenarios are these. `inbox`: the app as it opens, no view opened. `one`: one pane on the stage. `eight-in-turn`: eight panes in two work items, each put in front once, the last one left on the stage. `four`: four panes side by side. `four-dom`: `four` with WebGL refused to the page's canvases from the debugging port, so that the app's own fallback keeps the DOM renderer. `four-after-eight`: the panes of `eight-in-turn` each shown once, then four of them side by side. `inbox-after-four`: `four`, then the inbox screen in front. Each scenario is a launch of its own.
   - It prints one table row per scenario and run, and for the last sample of each run the footprint of each process by its kind: main, renderer, GPU, utility, daemon, and `other` for a process below the main process that names no type. It also prints the commit it built, and for each run the sizes of the window, of the stage and of each terminal on it, with the columns and rows of the terminal in front. It ends through `BuiltApp#stop` and removes its repository and its worktrees folder.
7. **The number and its method are written in one file**, `apps/desktop/measurements/memory.md`: the date, the commit, the machine and its displays with their scale, the versions of macOS, Electron and xterm.js, the window's size and a tile's size in pixels and in columns and rows, what counts as the app itself and what is summed apart, the size of 1 MB, the command, the kind of start, and the measurement's tables.
   - The criterion's number is the highest sample of scenario `four` over three runs. The file says in its first lines whether that number is at or under 500 MB.
   - The runs this work publishes are an agent's, under `OWLERY_BACKGROUND=1` with the emulated focus. The file says so, names the three things such a start lacks against a person's (the Dock icon, the menu bar and the system's focus), and gives the command of a person's run. This work makes no person's run (`frame/questions.md` Q5).
   - The changelog fragment carries the number and the file's path.
8. **The window names no vendor**, as today, and the new message carries none.
9. Out of scope, each with its reason:
   - A change of what a person sees that would lower the number: the DOM renderer in place of WebGL, a smaller window at start, or a lower cap than four. The measurement publishes `four-dom` beside `four`, and the choice is the owner's (`frame/questions.md` Q3).
   - The layout made for one orchestrator with parallel subtasks, or for parallel features. The owner sent it to issue #35 (`## Grounding`).
   - A screen state the daemon parses and keeps for each terminal, in place of the raw output it replays. `docs/architecture.md` *Measure before building* asks for the daemon's CPU cost of parsing output to be measured first, and nobody has. `frame/plan.md` *Alternatives considered* holds the row.

The per-process tables give the WebGL and the DOM number at this app's tile size on the machine that runs the measurement, and no more.
   - A cap on the resources of hidden terminals, the startup time and the install size (`docs/roadmap.md` *Open decisions*, the owner's after this measurement).
   - Dragging a split, resizing a tile, tiles of more than one work item, and keeping the layout across a reload.

## Data & interfaces

In `apps/desktop/src/shared/protocol.ts`:

- Client: new `{ type: "detach"; id: number }`. It has no answer.
- `exit` stays as it is. The window reads it for nothing it draws.

In `apps/desktop/src/daemon/`: `daemon.ts` handles `detach` by taking the session out of the set of that client (`Daemon#clients`), and at a process's exit appends the exit line through the path a pane's own output takes, in `Daemon#startSession`, where the exit also clears issue #23's `silentStart`. `buffer.ts#append` drops the rest of the line a cut falls in, through its line break, where that line break stands within the first 4,096 characters kept. It drops nothing more where the cut falls at a line's start or the line break stands past them. `daemon.ts` takes a `resize` only for a session whose process runs: the pseudo-terminal of a process that exited is closed, and a size set on it throws. Their cases go in `apps/desktop/test/daemon.test.ts`, where the buffer's and the replay's cases are.

In `apps/desktop/src/renderer/`:

- The decisions are pure functions with cases in `apps/desktop/test/`, as `view.ts` holds the window's others: the panes on the stage (scenario 2), the screens they take and give back (scenario 3), and which replay is written (scenario 6). Which pane a screen sends to is `view.ts#screenTurns`, which keeps the screen's turns in the order of its terminal's writes and is handed that terminal, so its cases run without a page. Which closed panes stay among the panes that were in front is `view.ts#recentKept`: the first of them, the pane last in front, stays, so the live pane with the highest id follows a closed pane that was in front, and not the pane in front before it. Where "Side by side" is pressed, `view.ts#stageOf` gives that closed pane no place: the stage holds the first four of the stage order (*Scope* item 1), and the live pane of those four with the highest id follows. Where fewer than four of the other tabs were in front, those four include tabs that were never in front, and such a tab can be the pane that follows. Where none of those four runs, the one of them with the highest id follows, an exited pane, even where a live pane stands off the stage, because *Scope* item 1 names the four for the stage and the pane in front stands on it. `paneTabs` loses its `held` parameter. The builder names the functions and may give them a module of their own beside `view.ts`; a name is a line of the phase record.
- `work-item-view.ts` holds the pool in place of `panes`, its map of one terminal per pane, and builds the tiles. What it gives the rest of the window follows: `onStage` answers whether a pane is on the stage, which `renderer.ts#takeLists` reads for a notification; `dropClosed` gives a closed pane's screen back; `leaveView` gives every screen back; `startSize` and the strip's size read the screen that has the keyboard; `showTerminalColours` reaches every screen of the pool; and the stage's `ResizeObserver` fits every screen on the stage.
- `renderer.ts#onMessage` loses the line it writes at `exit`.
- `renderer.ts#onMessage` sends `detach` at `started` for a pane that did not come onto the stage.
- The window sends no `resize` for a pane whose process exited, so a daemon left running by a build before `38dce05` does not fail when an exited pane's tab is pressed. Phase 4 builds it.
- `index.html` gains the "Side by side" button in `#pane-bar` and the stage's rules for the tiles.
- A screen's element stays a `.pane` under `#stage` and carries `hidden` while it is off the stage. `apps/desktop/test/korean-pane.ts` and `korean-input.test.ts` find the terminal in front as `#stage .pane:not([hidden])` and wait for one `.xterm-helper-textarea`, and the Korean case never presses "Side by side".

The measurement: `apps/desktop/test/memory.ts` holds its functions and its driving of the window, as `korean-pane.ts` holds the Korean case's, and `apps/desktop/test/memory.test.ts` holds the cases and, under `describe.runIf`, the measurement. `apps/desktop/test/fixtures/` gains the panes' fixture. `apps/desktop/package.json` gains the script `measure:memory`. The scenarios to run and the count of runs reach the measurement as environment variables: `OWLERY_MEMORY_SCENARIOS`, the scenarios' names separated by commas, every scenario where it is absent, and `OWLERY_MEMORY_RUNS`, the number of runs, three where it is absent. `apps/desktop/measurements/memory.md` is written by the builder from the measurement's output.
