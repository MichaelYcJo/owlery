## Not done

Three launches of each scenario were not made in phase 1. The spawn asked for as few starts of the app as the phase needs on a machine its owner works at, and the phase's comparison, eight terminals against one, does not need them.

`korean-pane.ts#openReceiverPane` and `memory.ts#openWorkItem` both fill the "New work item" form, and no shared function was made of them. It would change the Korean case's file, which this phase's plan row does not name, and the two differ in what they wait for: the receiver's file, or the tab and its terminal.

The measurement prints the date, the versions of macOS, Electron and xterm.js, the commit it built, and for each run the sizes of the window, of the stage and of each terminal on it with the columns and rows of the one in front. It does not print the machine or its displays. `apps/desktop/measurements/memory.md` holds them as `system_profiler SPHardwareDataType SPDisplaysDataType` gave them by hand.

A launch that is thrown away is not made again by the tool. The run fails, and whoever ran it runs it again.

The cut at a line's start reads at most the first 4,096 characters kept for a line break, on each append that drops output (`apps/desktop/src/daemon/buffer.ts#dropLineRest`, the bound of `frame/design.md` *Scope* item 5). Phase 2 measured 29 microseconds an append for pieces of 64 characters where the search read all 256K characters kept. The bounded search was not timed: it reads at most 4,096 characters whatever is kept.

`eight-in-turn` after the pool was launched once in phase 3, not three times, for the reason phase 1's three scenarios were: the fall from eight terminals to one shows on one launch.

Phase 4 made thirteen launches and not the twenty-one of three for each of seven scenarios. `four`, `four-dom` and `four-after-eight` have three each: the criterion's number is the highest of three runs of `four`, the owner reads `four-dom` beside it, and scenario 11 holds `four-after-eight` within 5% of `four`. `inbox`, `one`, `eight-in-turn` and `inbox-after-four` have one each, since nothing is read from them within a margin a second launch could move. The spawn asked for as few starts of the app as the work needs on a machine its owner works at.

Nothing was changed to bring `four` under 500 MB, where its highest sample is 503.4 MB. The owner chose that the numbers alone are published (`frame/questions.md` Q3, answer (a)). That answer leaves room for a change that alters nothing a person sees where the per-process table points at one. The table points at the GPU process, about 320 MB of the 500, which holds about 304 MB for one terminal as well, and no change of that kind was found there.

Nothing answers a question a program asks its terminal while its pane is off the stage. The pane holds no screen then, and when it comes back the answers to its replay are sent only where that replay is the first the window draws of it since the window loaded (`apps/desktop/src/renderer/work-item-view.ts#write`). Before this work a hidden pane kept its terminal, which answered at once. The other two choices were weighed and not taken: sending every replay's answers types each old answer into the pane again at every press of its tab, which phase 3's first drive read, and sending none leaves a pane's first questions unanswered where it printed them before the window asked for its output. A screen state the daemon keeps is what would answer them, and `frame/design.md` *Scope* item 9 puts it out of scope. Phase 4's drive read the cost once: three panes that each left the stage within a moment of their start, before their process had asked where the cursor is, were sent no answer in that load of the window, and were sent it at their first replay after a reload.

While the terminal draws a later replay, or still draws what it was handed before the pane on the screen took it, what a person types into that screen is not sent, because xterm.js hands the window a person's keys and the terminal's answers the same way (`apps/desktop/src/renderer/view.ts#screenTurns`, review round 3's finding 1). Neither span was timed.

The window holds back the size of a screen whose pane the daemon lists as exited (`apps/desktop/src/renderer/view.ts#tellsSize`). Between the moment a pane's process ends and the moment the window learns of it, a size sent for that pane could still reach a closed pseudo-terminal in a daemon built before `38dce05`; nothing catches that, as nothing did before the work. The daemon of this branch takes such a size and changes nothing.

A launch fails where `footprint` gave no number for any process of the app (`apps/desktop/test/memory.ts#unread`, round 1's finding 1), and no process of the app is let off. A helper that Chromium starts and ends between the read of `ps` and the read of `footprint` would fail a launch that way. No launch has been made since the rule, so nobody has seen whether one does. The by-kind rows of phase 1's three kept launches hold the same five kinds (`trail/phases/phase-1.md`).
