# Changelog: four terminals on screen, and the app's memory measured

Issue #9. The target of about 500 MB for the app itself was a number from a bare Electron window, and nobody had measured it in the app. The window also kept a terminal for every pane a person had ever shown, so its memory grew with each tab visited, and it could show one terminal at a time.

- **Four terminals side by side read 503.4 MB, which is over the target of about 500 MB.** That is the highest sample of three runs, whose samples lie between 491.1 and 503.4 MB, on an M3 Pro MacBook with two 4K displays, in a window of 1440 by 900. `apps/desktop/measurements/memory.md` holds the number, its method and every table. The runs are an agent's, with the app started in the background. Nobody has yet made the run a person makes, and what follows a number over the target is the owner's decision.
- **"Side by side" shows up to four of a work item's panes at once.** The button stands in the bar over the terminals. Pressed, the work item's panes stand as tiles: two in two columns, and three or four in two columns and two rows, the third of three spanning both. A work item with more tabs shows the four that were most recently in front. A press on a tab that is not shown puts its pane in place of the one least recently in front, and a press inside a tile gives that pane the keyboard. The control is off each time the window loads. At the window's opening size a tile is 49 columns by 27 rows.
- **A tab says whether its pane is on screen.** The tab of each pane on screen stands on the terminal's ground, and the tab of the pane that has the keyboard has the edge and the strong ink as well.
- **No notification is shown for a pane you are looking at,** whether or not it has the keyboard, while the window has the focus.
- **The window holds a terminal only for a pane on screen.** A pane that leaves the screen hands its terminal to the next pane, and the daemon keeps its output. When the pane comes back, the window draws that output again. Eight panes each shown once read 480.3 MB, where they read 764.5 MB before.
- **An exited pane keeps its tab and its last screen,** across a reload of the window too, until you close it. Its output ends with `[process exited with code <N>]`, which the daemon now writes.
- **A pane's output that comes back opens at the start of a line.** Where the daemon drops old output, it also drops the rest of the line the cut falls in, unless the next line break is more than 4,096 characters away.
- **A program that asks its terminal a question while its pane is not on screen gets no answer.** A terminal answers such questions, the place of the cursor among them, only while it draws them. The questions in a pane's output are answered the first time the window draws that output after it loads, and not when the pane comes back later. For a moment after a pane comes onto the screen, and while its earlier output is drawn again, what you type into it is not sent.
- **`pnpm run measure:memory`, from `apps/desktop`, takes the measurement.** It builds the app, starts it once for each scenario and run, opens shell panes that print about 300 lines a second, and reads macOS's `footprint` for the app's processes 20, 25 and 30 seconds after the last step. It needs macOS. `OWLERY_MEMORY_SCENARIOS` names the scenarios and `OWLERY_MEMORY_RUNS` the runs of each. An agent runs it with `OWLERY_BACKGROUND=1`; a person leaves that unset and keeps the window in front.
- **For a client of the daemon:** `detach`, with a session's `id`, ends that client's receiving of the session's output until it sends `attach` again, and is answered with nothing. A session that exited stays on the lists, with its output, until a client sends `close`. A `resize` for a session whose process exited changes nothing.
- **A daemon left running by an earlier build goes on working with the new window,** with two differences until it exits: it sends a hidden pane's output, which the window drops, and it writes no exit line.
