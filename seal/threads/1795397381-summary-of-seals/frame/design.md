# Design: 1795397381-summary-of-seals

Issue #22: a person who comes back sees no summary of what changed while they were away. Release 0.0.1 built the summary screen, the daemon's record of the person's last look and the count of work items that wait (#18, built by #37). This work adds what needs a seal: a seal given or broken since the look opens the summary, and the summary leads with those work items, each with its seal.

This frame keeps the words of the frame it builds on in the sense that frame gives them: a **seal**, the **sealed commit**, and a seal that **holds** or is **broken**, of `seal/threads/1795396351-seal-breaks-after-a-commit/frame/design.md`. It adds five, each used in one sense. The **look** is the daemon's one record of when a person last looked, with the lists of that moment (`apps/desktop/src/shared/protocol.ts#Look`). A work item has a **seal reading** on a side, the look's or the present one, where it carries the field `seal` there, whether the field holds a seal or null. A seal was **given since the look** where the work item's present seal is `sealed` and the look does not hold that work item `sealed` at the same commit. A seal **broke since the look** where the present seal is `broken` and the look does not hold that work item `broken` at the same commit. The **changed set** is the work items the window lists as changed since the look, worked out when it loads.

## Scope

### The daemon and the look

1. **A work item carries no `seal` until the daemon has read its branch.** #44's build sends null there (`daemon.ts#workItemOf` at `afa0566`). `workItemOf` leaves the field `seal` out of a work item for which the map `branches` holds no entry: one whose branch the daemon has not read since it started, one with no base commit recorded, and one whose branch is gone. Null then says that the daemon read the head commit and found no seal there. It still says so where git failed at that read, as #44's `daemon.ts#sealAt` logs and this work leaves. `protocol.ts` says both at the field. A window reads an absent `seal` as no seal where it draws the seal's place, which `view.ts#sealShown` already does at `afa0566`. An archived work item follows the same rule: the daemon reads no archived work item's branch, so after a restart such a work item carries no `seal` where #44's build sent null, and the window reads both as no seal. No look and no summary holds an archived work item (phase 1, `trail/phases/phase-1.md`).
2. **The look keeps each work item's seal reading.** `last-look.ts#lookOf` copies `seal` where the work item carries one: the state, the sealed commit and the sentence. It keeps a null as null and leaves an absent one absent. `#isWorkItem` takes an absent `seal`, a null and a `Seal`, and a file whose `seal` is anything else is no look. `#CameLater` names the field. A look written by a build before this work holds no seal reading for any work item, and #44's build reads such a look already (item 4).

### What counts as a change

3. **A seal given or broken since the look makes its work item a changed work item.** `view.ts#changesSince` reads the seal of each side. The table gives the answer for a work item both sides hold with a seal reading.

   | The look holds | Now `sealed` at commit A | Now `broken`, sealed at commit A | Now null |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | null | given | broke | nothing of the seal |
   | `sealed` at A | nothing of the seal | broke | nothing of the seal |
   | `sealed` at another commit | given | broke | nothing of the seal |
   | `broken`, sealed at A | given | nothing of the seal | nothing of the seal |
   | `broken`, sealed at another commit | given | broke | nothing of the seal |

   Where the answer is "nothing of the seal", the work item is changed or not by what `changesSince` reads today. So a seal that left the head, as after a reset or a newer verdict that withholds, is said by the line's state and by no words of the seal. One more case answers "nothing of the seal": a seal that broke on a work item whose pull request merged. `view.ts#branchLine` at `afa0566` has a merged pull request say more than a broken seal, since a commit on the branch after the merge asks nothing of the person, and the summary follows it.
4. **A side without a seal reading says nothing of the seal.** Where the look's work item or the present one carries no `seal`, the seal is neither given nor broken, and the side without a reading is read with the other side's seal where the two lines' states are compared. #44's build does this for the look's side (`view.ts:1233` at `afa0566`), and this work adds the present side, for the comparison alone: the line the summary shows is the present work item's as it stands. So a daemon that just started, a look written before this work, and a look taken before the daemon's first read each open no summary by the seal alone. This is the rule `docs/product.md` *Foundation* states for a count of new commits.
5. **A work item the look does not hold counts its seal as since the look.** It is new since the look, as today. Where it carries a seal reading, a `sealed` one is given and a `broken` one broke.
6. **The summary opens on such a change as it opens on any other.** The changed set is worked out at `ready`, and the app opens on the summary where the set holds a work item (`renderer.ts:1455` at `afa0566`). This work adds no other moment at which the summary opens by itself. `frame/questions.md` Q1 holds the owner's open question on the other moments.

### What the summary says

7. **A change's sentence opens with the seal where the seal was given or broke.** In place of the words of the line's state, the sentence opens with "Sealed at `<commit>` after `<N>` review rounds." where the seal was given, and with "Seal broken." and the seal's own sentence, its `why`, where it broke. The commit is shown by its first seven characters, with no mark around it, as `view.ts#sealShown` says it at `afa0566`, and "Seal broken" is that module's constant. The count of rounds is `recorded.rounds`, and the words after the commit are left out where the daemon sent no `recorded` or the count is 0. What follows is as today: the new commits since the look, and the decisions made without asking. The line's icon still shows the line's state, which is the pull request's where the work item holds one (`view.ts#branchLine`).
8. **The lines lead with the seals.** The order is: the work items whose seal was given since the look, then the ones whose seal broke since the look, then the others in the order of the inbox's groups, as today. Inside each of the first two the work items keep the order the daemon lists them in.
9. **The summary's sentence counts the seals first, and each work item once.** A work item whose seal was given is counted as sealed, one whose seal broke as a broken seal, and every other changed work item by its line's state, as today: "2 sealed, 1 seal broken, 1 needs you, 1 working". The decisions made without asking follow after a full stop, as today. `view.ts#summaryHead` and the sidebar's count read the same changes.
10. **Each line carries its seal as it stands now.** #44's build already draws the place from the change's work item (`summary.ts:50` at `afa0566`), which is the work item as the load held it. With item 12 that work item is the one the window holds at the drawing, so this item asks for no code beyond item 12's, and scenario 12 holds it.
11. **The main control says why the work item it opens waits.** It opens the first line, in the screen's order, whose work item waits on an open question, a call to allow or a pane that waits, and says "Answer the first question". Where no line waits so and one waits for another reason, as for a broken seal, a stopped chain or a failed check of its pull request, it opens the first such line and says "Open the first that needs you". The inbox stands beside it in both, and is the main control where no line waits, as today.

### The summary says the present

12. **The changed set is the load's, and what each line says is the present.** `shared.ts#held` keeps the ids of the changed set, worked out at `ready`, in place of the list of changes. At each drawing of the summary, and for the sidebar's count, the window works out each change again from the look it was given at its hello and the lists it holds now. A work item of the set stays in it though nothing of it differs from the look any more, and its sentence then says its state alone. A work item of the set that the lists no longer hold, as after an archive, has no line.
13. **A seal the daemon read after `ready` joins the set once.** A work item that `ready` carried without a seal reading joins the changed set at the first lists that carry its reading, where its seal was then given or broke since the look. So a daemon that just started, whose `ready` went out before git answered, still gets its seals into the summary: one reading took 612 milliseconds in #44's phase 1, against the 500 `ready` waits (*Grounding*). The screen in front does not change for it: the sidebar's count says it, and the summary is one step away. No other change after `ready` joins the set until the next load.
14. **The summary in front is redrawn at each `inbox`.** `renderer.ts#takeLists` draws the summary again where it is the screen in front, without moving the keyboard's focus. So a seal that breaks while the summary is open turns its line's seal to the broken form, its sentence to "Seal broken.", and the count in the summary's sentence with them.

## Data & interfaces

**`apps/desktop/src/shared/protocol.ts`.** `WorkItem.seal?: Seal | null`, with #44's `Seal`, both as `afa0566` declares them. This work changes what the field's absence and its null say, and adds no field and no message.

- Absent: the daemon holds no reading of the work item's branch, or the daemon is of an earlier build.
- Null: the daemon read the head commit and found no seal, which is also what it sends where git failed at that read.

**`apps/desktop/src/daemon/`.**

- `daemon.ts#workItemOf` adds `seal` only where the map `branches` holds an entry for the work item.
- `last-look.ts`: `isSeal`, the field in `isWorkItem`, in `CameLater` and in `lookOf`. `lookOf` copies the values of a seal, keeps null and leaves an absent field out of the copy.

**`apps/desktop/src/renderer/view.ts`.** The functions stay pure. The names are the build's to choose.

- One function gives what changed of a seal between a look's work item and the present one: given, broke or nothing, by *Scope* items 3 to 5. `changeOf`, which `changesSince` calls, keeps the line of `afa0566` that reads a look without a seal with the present one, and gains its twin for the present side (phase 2).
- One function gives the change of one work item against the look, whether or not anything differs. `changesSince` is that function over every work item, keeping the ones that changed, in the order of *Scope* item 8.
- One function gives the changes of a set of ids from the look and the present lists, in the same order, for *Scope* item 12.
- One function gives the changed set after new lists: the set, and each work item of *Scope* item 13 that joins it.
- `Change` gains what changed of the seal. `summarySentence`, `summaryHead` and `summaryControls` take what they need of a change to count the seals and to tell why a line waits.

**`apps/desktop/src/renderer/`.** `shared.ts#held` keeps the changed set's ids and which work items `ready` carried without a seal reading. `summary.ts#showSummary` works the changes out at each drawing, so the seal it already passes to `sealPlace` is the present one, and has a way to draw again that leaves the focus. `summary.ts#pressMain` and the main control's listener read the same changes. `shared.ts#changedSince` reads the set's ids. `renderer.ts` works the set out at `ready`, and `takeLists` lets a late reading join it and draws the summary again where it is in front. `sidebar.ts` counts the lines the summary would draw.
